# Security Global Parity & Differentiation Matrix

> **Status:** Product-readiness evidence
> **Purpose:** Ensure Family OS meets meaningful global expectations without copying competitors, overpromising platform capabilities, or losing its connected-family advantage.

## 1. Comparison method

This is a product-outcome comparison, not a screen-for-screen imitation. A global family expects controls to be dependable, understandable, and specific to the device they are using. Family OS must match that expectation while differentiating through family coordination, learning context, child clarity, and truthful delivery states.

The evidence baseline uses official public materials for Google Family Link, Apple Screen Time/Family Sharing, Microsoft Family Safety, Qustodio, Bark, Life360, and Apple Screen Time APIs. Platform support and commercial offers can change; all parity claims must be rechecked at implementation and release time.

## 2. Outcome parity matrix

| Family outcome | Global expectation evidenced by alternatives | Family OS approved direction | Sequencing / truth boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healthy digital routine | Daily limits, schedules/downtime, app limits, requests, allowed essentials. | Time & Routine Desk with guardian/child request loop, schedules, exceptions, confirmed state. | Core; native/device enforcement is capability-gated. |
| Intentional app access | Allow/block, app categories, install approval, app-specific limits. | App Desk and app-review loop with child explanation and receipt. | Core; depends on trustworthy device app inventory/control support. |
| Safer web use | Category filtering, safe-search/content settings, exceptions; scope differs by system. | Web Desk with supported-surface statement, exceptions and respectful block/request experience. | Core UX; enforcement claim only per actual supported context. |
| Immediate parent response | Pause/lock/routine switch, but with essential-access constraints. | Immediate action with preview, delivery receipt, expiry/reversal and essential access treatment. | Core commitment; never universal “device lock” wording. |

## 3. Family OS differentiation

Family OS should not compete by making the longest list of controls. Its defensible experience is the combination of:

1. **One family context.** Safety, location, learning, tasks, communication, device health, and family roles share the same child/device identity.
2. **Truthful control.** A parent sees desired, queued, confirmed, limited, or failed—not a cosmetic toggle.
3. **Child clarity.** Every core restriction has an understandable child explanation and, when appropriate, a fair request route.
4. **Guardian collaboration.** Co-guardian authority is explicit, changes are traceable, and urgent events show response ownership.
5. **Safety with growth.** School/focus and education-aware routines connect protection to child development instead of treating time only as a limit.
6. **Quiet intelligence.** Signals and reports provide explanation and a human decision, rather than opaque automated punishment.
7. **One recovery experience.** Device health, permission repair, support, privacy, and audit are designed into the product rather than hidden in technical support.

## 4. Claims Family OS must not make before proof

The following phrases or equivalents are prohibited from product copy until real device and operations proof exists:

- “Works on every app / browser / device.”
- “Blocks everything instantly.”
- “Always knows your child’s live location.”
- “SOS guarantees emergency response.”
- “Monitors all messages/photos/social platforms.”
- “Detects every attempt to bypass controls.”
- “Detects every crash or distracted-driving event.”
- “Protection is active” when the selected device is offline, unsupported, stale, or needs a required permission.

## 5. Global product readiness requirements

Before market positioning, launch materials, or pricing uses any security feature, the team verifies:

- Country availability and language readiness.
- Android/iOS support matrix by meaningful feature, not a generic logo list.
- Family role/age/cultural flexibility in experience and support language.
- Accessibility and RTL/LTR parity.
- Time zone, travel, and daylight-saving handling for schedules/places/notifications.
- Data processing, visibility, retention, consent, and support operating model for the feature.
- Customer-support and incident-response capability appropriate to the promise.

## 6. Product positioning recommendation

The initial position is:

> **Family OS gives a family one calm place to guide digital routines, understand protection health, respond when it matters, and help each child grow.**

It is deliberately broader and more connected than a traditional parental-control app, while remaining more truthful and focused than a generic all-in-one family dashboard.
